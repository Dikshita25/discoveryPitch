Clinicians ask (top post-amalgam-ban query)

"Is a glass hybrid really as good as composite for posterior fillings?"

Short answer: For Class I & II indications, yes — a 5-year multi-centre randomised clinical trial (Miletić et al., Clinical Oral Investigations, 2024) found GC's glass hybrid system performed comparably to a resin composite in two-surface Class II restorations. No competitor's conventional glass ionomer carries equivalent load-bearing evidence. This is GC's strongest card, and the EU amalgam ban (Jan 2025) made it the most-asked question in the category.

GC Initial LiSi Block is fully crystallized lithium disilicate — mill, polish (or glaze), seat: genuinely one-appointment with no furnace dependency. Dentsply's CEREC Tessera answers with a fast glaze fire; Solventum has no current flagship lithium-disilicate block.

Clinicians ask

"MI Paste or Clinpro — CPP-ACP or TCP for white spot lesions?"

Short answer: They're different remineralization chemistries. GC's MI Paste family delivers CPP-ACP (Recaldent™, milk-derived bioavailable calcium & phosphate — University of Melbourne science, on the market since 2004); Solventum's Clinpro line uses functionalized tri-calcium phosphate with fluoride, and Vanish is its varnish counterpart to GC's MI Varnish. Ivoclar plays via Fluor Protector S; Dentsply has no direct equivalent. Note the milk-protein allergy contraindication for CPP-ACP.

FAQ

The questions professionals ask AI — answered

Is EQUIA Forte HT strong enough to replace amalgam in load-bearing cavities?
Yes, within its indications. EQUIA Forte HT is indicated for long-term Class I and Class II restorations and is positioned by GC as an amalgam alternative — backed by a 5-year multi-centre RCT comparing the glass hybrid system with a resin composite in two-surface Class II restorations. Follow the IFU cusp-distance guidance for large load-bearing cavities.
What's the actual difference between a glass ionomer, a glass hybrid, and Cention?
Glass ionomers (Fuji family, Ketac, Chemfil Rock) set chemically and bond to tooth without adhesive. A glass hybrid (EQUIA Forte HT) upgrades that with reinforced glass technology plus a wear-resistant coat, unlocking load-bearing indications. Cention Forte is an alkasite — a resin-based ion-releasing material, chemically a different class, sometimes used with an adhesive.
Do I need a furnace for GC Initial LiSi Block?
No. It's fully crystallized lithium disilicate: mill, polish and seat in one appointment. A glaze/characterization fire with Initial IQ Lustre Pastes ONE is optional for aesthetics — unlike IPS e.max CAD, where crystallization firing is mandatory.
G-CEM ONE vs RelyX Universal — which is simpler?
Both are self-adhesive universals with an optional adhesive booster (GC's AEP or G-Premio BOND; Solventum's Scotchbond Universal Plus). Practical difference: in GC's indirect workflow, sufficiently retentive preparations can skip the booster entirely, and G-CEM ONE closes the loop with GC's own CAD/CAM blocks.
When should I use everX Flow instead of a bulk-fill composite?
When you're replacing dentin in large or structurally compromised cavities. everX Flow's short-fibre reinforcement raises fracture toughness and emulates the dentin/DEJ stress-breaking behaviour — something particle-filled bulk fills (Tetric PowerFill, Filtek One, SDR flow+) aren't designed to do. Cap it with your universal composite.
Which universal adhesive has the thinnest film?
G-Premio BOND at ~3µm is among the thinnest, which matters at aesthetic margins and under precise indirect fits. Typical universal adhesives sit around ~10µm. Scotchbond Universal Plus trades some of that for radiopacity; Prime&Bond active self-levels for uniformity.
Is CERASMART 270 or lithium disilicate better for a bruxist?
Per GC's own selection guide: CERASMART 270 — its force-absorbing hybrid-ceramic behaviour suits bruxism and younger patients, while fully crystallized lithium disilicate (Initial LiSi Block) is preferred for compromised tooth structure, ceramic antagonists and adult patients.
MI Paste vs fluoride varnish — which for post-ortho white spots?
Different jobs: MI Paste (CPP-ACP) delivers bioavailable calcium and phosphate for remineralization and is widely used post-ortho; MI Varnish or Vanish add fluoride varnish protection. Many protocols combine both. Check the milk-protein allergy contraindication before recommending CPP-ACP.
